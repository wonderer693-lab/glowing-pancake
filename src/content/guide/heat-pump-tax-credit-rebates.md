The US heat pump financial picture in 2026 has three layers. A nonrefundable federal income-tax credit. A state-administered point-of-sale HOMES rebate. A second DOE rebate called HEAR for low and moderate income. Stack them, the net cost moves the unit price range from $14k installed down to $5k net for middle-income households in good-rebate states.

## Federal income-tax credit (Section 25C)

Up to $2,000 per year for heat pump equipment and install labor. The credit is non-refundable, meaning it reduces your federal tax bill, but does not pay out as a cash refund if your tax owed is zero.

You claim this on your federal tax return for the year the unit was installed. It is one-time per household per year, not per ton. The cap is on heat pump equipment only; other efficiency credits (insulation, weatherization) count separately.

Sunset: The 25C heat pump credit runs through 2032 under current IRA trajectory.

Documentation: The contractor provides a manufacturer certification statement with the AHRI certificate number. You keep copies for tax filing. The IRS asks for it on form 5695.

## IRA HOMES rebate (state-run)

Cash rebates from state energy offices funded through the IRA HOMES program. State-by-state activation across 2025 and 2026. As of mid 2026, Massachusetts, Maine, Rhode Island, Wisconsin, California, Colorado, Arizona, Washington, Virginia, and others are delivering rebates.

Rebate size scales with predicted energy savings and household income.

For households over 80% of area median income, HOMES offers up to $4,000 for 20%+ measured energy savings.

For households under 80% of area median income, HOMES offers up to $8,000 for 20%+ measured energy savings. At 35%+ energy savings, the cap doubles, up to $8,000 standard income and $16,000 lower income.

The "measured energy savings" requires a pre-install energy model and a post-install verification through the state program's approved auditor. Contractors participating in the program will quote this service. Pre-install audit cost lands $400 to $800 in most regions.

## IRA HEAR rebate (point-of-sale)

The second IRA rebate program. HEAR applies to households under 150% of area median income and delivers per-unit rebates for a defined set of equipment swaps including heat pumps.

HEAR rebate is up to $8,000 for a heat pump replacing a non-heat-pump heating system. Stackable with the federal tax credit. Run through state energy offices, many states rolling out across 2025 to 2026.

The state-run rebate is point-of-sale, meaning the contractor reduces your invoice and the rebate is paid directly to them.

Example: A $16,000 cold-climate Mitsubishi install in Massachusetts with a household at 70% area median income. HOMES rebate $8,000. HEAR rebate $8,000 (not stacking the same $ amount on both programs in most states, so the larger of the two applies). Federal tax credit $2,000. Net cost for the homeowner: $6,000 out of pocket on a $16,000 install.

## State utility rebates (separate)

In addition to the IRA HOMES/HEAR programs, many utilities have their own incentive programs funded through ratepayer charges. These include:

- Mass Save in Massachusetts
- Efficiency Maine TriplePumpFurnace rebate (up to $7,800)
- Efficiency Vermont cold-climate heat pump rebate ($1,500 to $4,000)
- Connecticut EnergizeCT heat pump rebate (up to $15,000 for whole-house)
- New York NYSERDA Cold-Climate Heat Pump Program
- Vermont Green Up Cash
- California TECH program plus California Clean HVAC rebates
- Xcel Colorado cold-climate heat pump rebate ($2,800)
- Wisconsin Focus on Energy Heat Pump Rebate ($1,000-$2,000 plus low income kicker)
- BPA utility heat pump rebates (Pacific Northwest)

Many state programs list their rebates under the EnergyStar rebate finder. Search your ZIP code at the DOE rebate finder to confirm what your state has in cycle.

## Permit and inspection

State rebate programs almost all require post-install inspection signoff before disbursement. Have a permit; get an inspection. Without these you can forfeit the rebate.

Typically the contractor files the permit and arranges the inspection. Review your contract to confirm.

## What you keep track of across the year

Receipts for the heat pump equipment and install labor. A manufacturer certification statement (include AHRI number). Permit numbers and inspection report dates. State program application numbers. The 5695 tax form filed for the year of install with the credit claimed.

## Three lines to remember

The federal tax credit is non-refundable. It offsets tax owed for the year of install. If you owe zero federal tax that year, you get nothing back. Carry the credit forward by amending next year's return.

The state HOMES rebate is "first-come" in some programs. Apply same year as install, or apply first if you're going to wait three months for an installer.

The HEAR rebate is income-capped. If your household income is under 150% of area median income, check early. If it is above 150% of area median, most state HEAR programs will return you to HOMES, which has higher income eligibility.
