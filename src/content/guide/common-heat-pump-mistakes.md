Most heat pump under-performance traces back to one of five installer mistakes. The unit looks fine on the spec sheet, the rooms are warm most days, and the bill is heavier than it should be by 10 to 30 percent. None of these mistakes announce themselves. None get caught by the homeowner until either a service invoice or a year of bills shows the truth.

Here is what to look for.

## Mistake one. Bigger is better.

A contractor quoted 36k BTU for a 2,000 sqft house without running Manual J. The spec sheet said the unit was cold-climate certified. The unit arrived with a 4-ton compressor.

What goes wrong. The unit short-cycles. The compressor clicks on, ramps to high, satisfies the thermostat, clicks off. Three to five minutes on, ten to twenty minutes off. Inverter modulation does not get chance to do its job. Seasonal COP drops 10 to 15 percent. Defrost cycle frequencies increase because each cycle has to defrost the same coil that cold longer.

How to catch it before sign. The Manual J result should be in writing. If your contractor quotes by experience without one, ask for the Manual J or find another contractor.

How to catch it after.轮 the unit sounds. On shoulder-season days (5C outdoor), the unit should be running gently most of the time without ever fully stopping. If it cycles on and off more than 6 times per hour, it is oversized. Cutting down the modulation with smart thermostat cycle slowdown helps but is not the answer. The right answer is the right size unit.

## Mistake two. Wrong switchover for dual fuel

This is what trips up dual-fuel installs. The contractor sets the switchover at a 5C outdoor, because that's what his trainer taught him ten years ago. The heat pump only runs shoulder season. The furnace runs the deep winter. Savings over furnace only drop from projected 35 percent to 9 percent.

How to catch it before sign. Watch the installer set the thermostat for switchover. If they set it at 5C or above without looking at your electric and gas rates, they are using a default. Walk through the cost calculation with them. If you are cold climate with reasonable rates, switchover should sit between -12C and -3C.

How to catch it after. Look at the system operation log if your thermostat supports one. Nest logs by hour. Daikin One+ logs by cycle. If the furnace ran more than 200 hours in cold climates, run the calculation. Reset the switchover setting.

## Mistake three. Clogged filters hidden behind the head

The indoor head's filter panel looks clean. Pull it out and the mesh is gray with dust. Filter saturated since spring because the owner forgot.

What goes wrong. Induced airflow drops 15 percent. Compressor can't get enthalpy drop because the air side loses delta T. COP falls 8 to 15 percent. Indoor head coil slowly ices during shoulder season if outdoor coil runs. In summer the same thing cuts the cooling capacity by similar margin.

How to catch it before it matters. Pull the filter every month or two and feel the mesh. If it feels heavy with dust, rinse it. Takes one minute.

## Mistake four. Line-set vacuum done in 30 minutes

Most braze work has residual moisture during the braze. The moisture gets pulled out through the vacuum pump's deep vacuum. The carpenters can run their work. A proper micron vacuum takes 45 minutes to 2 hours depending on system volume. If your installer finishes the vacuum and charge in 30 minutes flat and the micron did not dip below 500, your refrigerant loop has moisture.

What goes wrong. Moisture in the refrigerant decomposes over years into acids. The acids eat the windings on the inverter compressor. Slow degradation in COP for 6 to 10 years until abrupt compressor failure.

How to catch before sign. Watch the vacuum record or get a written vacuum log. Reputable contractors log the readings.

How to catch it after. Bizarre on it you cannot. Refrigerant analysed at any service call can offer hints.

## Mistake five. Outdoor coil blocked by shrub or fence

This is the siting mistake. The shrubs you planted last summer, two years from now, have grown strong, leafy, and 8 inches from the condenser. The fence gets installed 4 inches away because the lot is tight.

What goes wrong. The unit re-circulates its exhaust. Effective outdoor coil temperature rises 2 to 5C during operation. COP drops by similar percent. The unit keeps running. The humidity of the trapped exhaust area causes more frequent defrost cycles.

How to catch before sign. Walk the planned placement with the contractor before mounting. Twelve inch minimum side clearance, three foot service clearance on at least one side.

How to catch after. Stand behind the fence or shrub during winter operation. If the air there is warmer than free atmosphere by more than 2-3C, you have recirculation.

## The pattern across all five

None of these mistakes stop the unit from heats the home. None of them cause an HVAC emergency. None of them require a callout for repair. They slowly cost the household 10 to 30 percent of the spec sheet COP in operating cost over years and add to compression wear.

Combined effect across all five mistakes possible in a single bad install: 25 to 40 percent extra annual operating cost plus a 10 to 30 percent reduction in unit life.

The pattern is: easy to do, hard to spot, expensive to fix. The best protection is a contractor who walks through the spec sheet and Manual J with you before installation, and who logs the install checks after. Get references from neighbors. Call three. Pick the one who answers the right questions.
