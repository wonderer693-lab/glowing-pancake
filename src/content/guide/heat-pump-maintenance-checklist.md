Maintenance tasks for a modern heat pump come down to four real jobs. One monthly, two yearly, one every five years. Plus a fifth task that eats your COP without you knowing until the bill arrives.

## Monthly tasks

Filter inspection. Indoor wall-mounted heads have a washable mesh filter behind the front panel. Slide out, vacuum or rinse, dry, replace. Four to six weeks is the cycle. Clogged filters cut COP by 8 to 15 percent.

Ducted air handlers have a disposable MERV filter at the return grille. Same cycle. Replace at $7 to $20 per filter.

If you remember nothing else from this page, remember the filter. Filter cleaning is responsible for 80 percent of heat pump long-term reliability.

## Yearly

Outdoor coil brush. In spring after pollen season ends, brush any accumulated dust and cottonwood seeds off the outdoor coil. Use a soft brush, brush along the fin direction so fins do not bend. Bent fins cost airflow and drop COP for the next cooling season.

Pressure check. Refrigerant pressure on a service manifold. The compressor runs the same job with less refrigerant if a small leak developed, but the COP slide is silent. A refrigerant pressure check catches slow leaks before they age the compressor. Expect the contractor to charge $80 to $150 for the visit.

Drain pan cleaning. Indoor head drain pan should be cleared annually. Stagnant water plus dust breeds mildew. Some units have a UV lamp option; otherwise simply empty the pan.

Air handler fan motor check on ducted systems. Listen for bearing noise. Replace bearings proactively at 12 years of service life typically; reactive replacement costs more.

## Every five years

Refrigerant charge top-off. The leak rate from properly brazed joints under modern pneumatic service port caps is typically 0.5 to 1.5 percent per year. Five years at 1 percent leak means 5 percent charge lost. The unit still works at 95 percent charge, COP drops 5 to 8 percent.

Top-off refrigerant costs $200 to $400. Use only manufacturer-supplied refrigerant cylinder. R-32 and R-454B service tools require F-Gas certification on the tech's hands.

While the technician is there. Have them also check reversing valve operation, defrost cycle timing, swamp thermistor accuracy on the indoor stagnation, and drain pan float switch operation. About $150 added labor time for an informative hour.

## Symptoms you should never ignore

Symptom: Heat pump runs constantly, indoor temperature drops, outdoor coil has visible frost on the lower third. Likely cause: defrost cycle not triggering. Call your installer.

Symptom: Indoor head delivers air significantly cooler than usual, outdoor unit vibrates normally, refrigerant pressure check shows micron reading. Likely cause: refrigerant charge low. Top off.

Symptom: Loud buzzing noise from outdoor unit at moderate temperatures (above 0C outdoor), unusual cold output from the indoor head almost immediately after startup. Likely cause: compressor lockout has tripped due to electrical issue, electric backup strip is running instead. Expensive electricity. Get tech out within a week.

Symptom: Monthly bill jumps 25 percent or more from same month prior year, equipment looks the same. Likely cause: filter clogged or refrigerant leak. Both check by maintenance tech.

Symptom: Ducted airflow drops 30 percent at registers vs last fall. Same cause as above plus possibly dirty fan motor.

## What kills heat pumps most often

Number one. Refrigerant leaks due to improper installation. Brazed joints under high pressures. Done wrong means tiny gaps leak over years. Anywhere from 1 to 3 percent of installs annually suffer this fate. Catching it early during the warranty period saves the compressor $2,000.

Number two. Filter starvation. A dirty filter combined with high filter dust temperature eventually ices the indoor coil. Ice expands at 1°C differential settings. The thaw causes water damage. Clean filter monthly, never let it become the filter chain reaction.

Number three. Failed drain pan float. When the drain pan overflows because the float switch stuck, water damages the wall behind the indoor head. Yearly drain pan cleaning prevents.

Number four. Outdoor coil salt corrosion in coastal climates. Salt coats the aluminum fins, oxygen attacks, fins disappear. Pad mounting 50 to 100 feet from ocean intake water 2 to 3 times the typical repair frequency. Pre-rinse the coil twice a year with fresh water using a garden hose.

Number five. Compressor failure from gross oversize. Short-cycled compressor burns the inverter board, ages the unit by 50 percent.

## The honest five-task summary

Wash or replace filters monthly.
Brush the outdoor coil in spring.
Pay for a refrigerant pressure check once a year.
Replace the charge if any signs suggest.
Brush down the outdoor unit twice a year if you live near salt spray.

Most failures get traced back to skipping one of these five. The maintenance contract the installer pitched you at $350 per year probably does this and not much else. You can do most of it yourself if you do not mind climbing a step ladder.

## Three lines to remember

Filter cleaning is 80 percent of heat pump longevity.

Refrigerant leaks are silent, only a yearly pressure check catches them.

A heat pump with a brand name you trust and a service habit you actually do will outlive two unattended units of any brand.
