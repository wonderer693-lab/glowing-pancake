If you have radiators or underfloor heating, your existing boiler moves water. A heat pump can do the same job. Heat pump plus hot water tank plus radiators is called a hydronic heat pump setup, or air-to-water. It works. It costs more upfront than a ducted air-to-air swap, mostly because almost nobody has done one in your neighborhood yet.

The boiler replacement decision differs from the furnace replacement decision in two ways. First, you are not just swapping the source of heat. You are also reconsidering how hot the water in your radiators needs to be. Second, you usually end up replacing the hot water tank at the same time, which is a bigger project than a furnace swap.

## Why the water temperature matters

Standard cast-iron radiators from before 1990 were sized to take 75C to 85C supply water from a cast iron or gas boiler. The heat output rating printed on the radiator assumes that temperature.

A heat pump producing 80C water has a COP of about 1.6. A heat pump producing 50C water has a COP of about 3.4. The boiler swap pays off mostly because of COP, so the water temperature decision drives the running cost. If you keep your old radiators and the old supply temperature, you halve the COP and pay about the same as the boiler did.

The right move, almost always, is to oversize your radiators or add fan convectors to lower the supply water to 50C to 55C. This is called low-temperature hydronic. It lets the heat pump run at COP 3 plus, which is the whole point.

## Two workable retrofit paths

Path one, cheap and reversible. Add a heat pump water cylinder in parallel with the existing boiler. The heat pump makes the water for the radiators at 50C, the cylinder keeps a buffer, and the boiler fires only on the coldest stretches. This costs around $14k installed and does not require radiator replacement. You cut gas use by 60 to 80 percent in a cold climate.

Path two, full retrofit. Replace the boiler, replace the radiators with low-temperature panel radiators or add underfloor loops where accessible, swap the hot water tank. Cost lands around $24k to $32k for a 3-bedroom house. You eliminate the boiler. Emissions drop to whatever your electricity produces, running cost drops to roughly half or less.

## What about combi boilers

Combi boilers heat both the radiators and the tap water on demand out of one unit. They were popular in UK housing stock through 2020. A heat pump cannot easily replace a combi because heat pumps do not do instant hot water at 75C plus. You need to add a hot water cylinder.

In a small UK kitchen, finding the space for a 200 liter cylinder is an honest project. Many homeowners end up putting the cylinder in a closet or the loft. The floor area cost is about one square meter.

## Three practical notes

First. If your radiators are over 30 years old, get a quote on replacement radiators anyway. Some old radiators are so internally corroded that they leak heat unevenly between rooms. Modern steel panel radiators with convector fins output 50 percent more heat at the same physical size and water temperature. That makes the low-temperature retrofit plausible without upsizing.

Second. Underfloor loops that were installed when the house was built are not necessarily good for heat pump supply water. Some old underfloor is plumbed in too few loops to spread heat at 50C. A hydronic survey costs about $400 and tells you what you actually have under the floor.

Third. Get a heat loss survey before any quote. Every heat pump retrofit starts with a room-by-room heat loss calculation. If your contractor skips this, cancel the appointment. The radiator and water temperature decision falls out of the heat loss survey, not the other way around.

## The honest answer for old houses

If your radiators are 40 years old and the boiler is on its last legs, the time to consider a heat pump retrofit is now. Air-to-water units from Vaillant, Viessmann, Daikin, and Bosch have all reached the point of being installation-ready. The UK BUS grant covers up to £7,500 of the install. The US IRA credit covers 30 percent up to $2,000. Either pot lowers the cost enough to be in the same league as a new boiler swap, with running cost on the heat pump side shooting further ahead each year as gas prices stay loose and electric gets cleaner.

The boiler is not quite the furnace when you ask the retrofit question, mostly because the water temperature and the radiator sizing make it a more involved project. But the math is moving the same direction. Heat pumps are winning in 2026, and they will win more in 2027.
