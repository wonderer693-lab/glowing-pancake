Total installed cost of a heat pump varies more by region than people realize. Sometimes the difference is double the price for the same hardware, depending on state. Sometimes the difference is in the rebates available after install.

This page shows the honest total install cost for one specific configuration, plus the available rebates after the install. Use it as a baseline; get three quotes from local installers and compare. If you want the brand-by-brand verdicts that go with these numbers, the [best heat pumps for 2026](/guide/best-heat-pumps-2026/) guide walks through them in detail. If you have not sized the unit yet, run the [heat pump sizing calculator](/guide/heat-pump-sizing-calculator/) first.

## The reference configuration

A cold-climate air-source split system for a typical 2,000-square-foot house with 36k BTU heating load at design temperature. Three indoor zones. Standard install, no electrical panel upgrade needed. Line-set under 30 feet.

Equipment options fall in three tiers:
- Mitsubishi MUZ-FS HyperHeat 36k multi-zone: $14,000 to $18,500 installed, before rebates
- Daikin Aurora 36k cold-climate multi-zone: $13,500 to $17,000 installed
- Bosch IDS 2.0 HVS 36k multi-zone: $10,500 to $13,500 installed

The [Mitsubishi review](/guide/mitsubishi-heat-pump-review/) and [Daikin review](/guide/daikin-heat-pump-review/) break down why those two price tiers exist and where the cheaper brands give ground.

Now by region.

## New England

Maine. Installed costs run $15,500 to $19,500 for Mitsubishi HyperHeat, $14,000 to $17,500 for Daikin Aurora, $11,000 to $13,500 for Bosch IDS HVS. Maine TriplePumpFurnace rebate offers up to $7,800 from Efficiency Maine. Combined federal and state brings Mitsubishi net to $5,700 to $10,000.

Massachusetts. Installed costs $15,500 to $19,500 Mitsubishi. Mass Save offers up to $10,000 back for low to moderate income. Combined with federal $2,000 credit, Mitsubishi net is $3,500 to $7,500. Mass Save Heat Loan offers 7-year 0 percent for the balance.

Vermont. Similar install pricing. Efficiency Vermont rebate up to $4,000. Net Mitsubishi $9,500 to $13,500.

New Hampshire, Connecticut, Rhode Island. EIA program listens now reduced relative to 2024. Federal credit only counts at the high end, so check utility-specific rebates.

## Mid-Atlantic

New York State. NYSERDA $1,500 rebate for cold-climate units plus Con Edison or Orange even more, depending on utility serving the address. Install pricing $14,500 to $19,000 Mitsubishi HyperHeat. Net after federal and NYSERDA around $11,000 to $15,500.

Pennsylvania. Pricing $14,500 to $19,000 Mitsubishi HyperHeat. PECO and PP anquire rebate up to $1,200. Net after federal around $11,300 to $15,800.

## Pacific

California. CCP rebate program is still rolling out across the investor-owned utilities. Pre-rebate cost sits $13,500 to $18,000 Mitsubishi HyperHeat. Post-Care EQ Tech rebates range $1,000 to $4,500 depending on CARE enrollment and climate zone.

Oregon and Washington state. Battery-rebate $2,000 Energy Trust typically offers cold-climate heat pump rebates of $1,500 to $3,500 for ducted. Net Mitsubishi $10,500 to $14,000.

## South and Southeast

Texas. Highest building volume in the US creates decent pricing pressure. Installed cost $10,500 to $15,500 Mitsubishi HyperHeat (without need at cold climate). Federal credit brings to $8,500 to $13,500.

Georgia, Florida, Louisiana. Similar pricing pattern for cold climate units, with $300 to $700 utility rebates typical. Net after federal $8,500 to $13,500.

## Midwest

Wisconsin, Minnesota, Michigan, Illinois. Pricing sits $14,500 to $19,500 Mitsubishi HyperHeat. State Energy Office rebates vary from $500 (Wisconsin Focus on Energy value tier) to $3,500 (Michigan Saves). Net Mitsubishi $11,000 to $16,500.

## Mountain West

Colorado. Continue Xcel Energy rebates at the meter level. Up to $2,800 cold-climate heat pump with Heat Pump Plus control tier. Installed $14,000 to $18,500 Mitsubishi HyperHeat. Net after federal and Xcel $9,200 to $13,700.

Utah ThermWise and Idaho rebates vary by $200 to $1,000.

## Final figure to expect

For a cold-climate 3-ton multi-zone Mitsubishi HyperHeat install, total out-of-pocket for a middle-income homeowner should land somewhere between $8,500 and $14,500 in most US states as of mid 2026. The same for Daikin Aurora lands $7,500 to $12,000. For Bosch IDS HVS, net $6,000 to $9,500.

The largest saving in selecting a unit comes from state rebate program cycles. If your state has a high rebate and your installers don't know it, you leave $1,500 to $10,000 on the table. Two hours of research on the state Efficiency Program page and you've captured the rebate without having paid it back through the installer. The full rebate stack by program is in the [heat pump tax credits and rebates](/guide/heat-pump-tax-credit-rebates/) guide.

## Three lines to remember

The federal credit does not expire when the IRA bill goes away. Only the state programs change cycle. The $2k federal tax credit is still good through 2032.

Some state programs count the install labor too. The federal credit only counts equipment. Add both up cleanly.

A higher-priced installer may include the pre-install energy audit and grid-weather-risk walk, which sometimes uncover issues that would have caused a service call within six months. Costs climb differently across installers. For the loan products that let you spread the net cost, [heat pump financing options](/guide/heat-pump-financing-options/) covers the common ones.
