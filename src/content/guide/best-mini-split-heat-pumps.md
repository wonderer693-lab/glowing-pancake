Mini-split is the architecture of choice for houses without ducts and rooms added to existing houses. One outdoor unit, one or more wall or ceiling heads, refrigerant lines fishing between them. The install is faster than ductwork, the cost is lower per zone, and the comfort is fine in any room where you can put a head.

## Single-zone outdoor unit

### Mitsubishi MUZ-FS single-zone

The cold-climate pick of the single-zone world. COP 2.2 at -8C works for one-room installs even in cold climates. One outdoor unit, one wall head, simple thermostat in the head, no Ductwork. Around $3,800 to $5,500 installed for a 1-ton unit.

### Daikin Series A single-zone

Daikin's mid-spec line. COP at -8C around 1.8, capacity at -15C 65 percent. Not cold-climate certified but does not pretend. Around $3,200 to $4,800 installed in mild climates.

### Senville Aura 12,000 BTU

The budget pick. Single-zone, 12,000 BTU, SEER2 around 19. Heats to -7C, COP at that point going down to 1.4 or so. Not a cold-climate unit. Fine for shoulder climates. Around $2,200 installed if you do the bracket and have someone certified braze the lines. Not fine in northern Vermont.

## Multi-zone outdoor unit

### Mitsubishi SUZ (multi-zone)

The cold-climate multi-zone for the cold-climate brands. SUZ paired with two, three, or four MSZ-GS indoor heads. As you add zones you trim warm output per zone because the compressor has to share-load. The full published capacities of each MSZ max out only if you run one head at a time. Two heads together gets you 80 to 90 percent of each. Three heads together drops to 70 percent each.

Cost runs $14,000 to $19,000 installed for a 3-zone cold-climate multi-split. Same as the air-source ducted equivalent.

### Daikin Aurora multi-zone

Same cold-climate spec story as single-zone. The 4-zone outdoor compressor shares load correctly down to -15C. COP across zones shifts as you add heads, predictably.

The wiring is more involved than single-zone, the freedom to set different temperatures per room is real. Around $13,500 to $17,500 installed for a 3-zone cold-climate Aurora.

### LG Multi F with cold-climate variant

LG's multi-zone line covers many indoor head types. They offer wall, ceiling-cassette, console, and concealed duct heads. The Red series cold-climate variants are real cold-climate units, COP 2.0 at -8C. The standard Multi F line sold heavily on Amazon Home Solutions is not cold-climate.

Installed cost runs $12,000 to $16,500 for a 3-zone cold-climate Red multi-zone setup. Good product. Service support is variable across regions; check before buying.

## Hidden ducted mini-split (concealed head)

A useful middle ground. One outdoor unit, one small concealed air handler inside a closet or ceiling that runs a short duct run into a few rooms. Mounts like a normal mini-split but with the head hidden.

The advantage: the room has no visible head, much quieter than wall-mounted, easier to integrate with central air filters and humidifiers.

Cost runs $6,500 to $10,000 installed for a 1.5-ton single concealed-head unit with a 2 to 3 register duct run. Higher than wall-mounted mini-split but lower than full ducted split with an air handler.

Best picks in this category are the Mitsubishi SEZ ceiling cassette, the Daikin CDZ concealed cassette, and the Bosch UB indoor air handler. All three live within the multi-zone architecture of their brands.

## Things mini-splits get wrong

The classic mistake. Four zones where two would work. People buy four heads because there are four rooms. Then they run one head at a time and the compressor idles all winter.

The fix. Walk through the rooms you actually use in winter. Usually two, sometimes three. Get a 2-zone or 3-zone outdoor unit and add a small fan-only head in the rooms you sometimes use. If you bought a 4-zone, hide one and use it during weekend.

The second mistake. Heads placed high on outer walls. Cold air off the head does not circulate into the room. Drop the head, ideally on the wall opposite the window. The diffuser pattern pushes the air across the room, balancing the cold from the window.

The third mistake. Noisy install on a wall adjacent to a bedroom. The compressor inside the wall head modulates around 25 to 35 dB. The way most people hear that depends on the wall. If the wall shares a bedroom or your office, you will hear it. Move the head to a different wall. Worth the extra $200 of copper.

## The honest mini-split recap

Mini-splits are not a budget hack. Done well they cost as much as a proper ducted system. The value is in the comfort of per-room control and the possibility of retrofitting a house without gutting for ducts. Buy Mitsubishi if you live cold, Bosch IDS or Daikin Aurora for value, and remember that the number of indoor heads is less important than the number of heads you actually use at once.
