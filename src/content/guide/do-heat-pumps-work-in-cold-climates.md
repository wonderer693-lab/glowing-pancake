The short answer is yes. The longer answer is that the unit you buy has to actually be a cold-climate unit. Most heat pumps sold in the US today are not. Calling a unit a heat pump does not make it cold-climate the way calling a tire a snow tire does not make it a snow tire.

## What "cold-climate" actually means

There is a real spec. AHRI, the industry testing body, issues cold-climate certification to units that meet performance targets at -5C and -15C. The key numbers are maximum capacity at -15C and COP at -15C. Most units marketed as cold-climate quote COP between 1.8 and 2.3 at -15C, and deliver 70% to 100% of nameplate heating capacity down to that temperature.

A standard, non-cold-climate unit typically quotes heating capacity down to -8C and falls off a cliff below that. The takeaway is not that standard units are bad, it is that the marketing tendency to call everything a heat pump does not help you. Read the spec sheet. Look for the words "cold climate" or a heating performance table that includes a -15C row with capacity and COP. If neither exists, the unit is not cold-climate.

## Brands that took cold climate seriously

As of 2026 the brands shipping verified cold-climate units include Mitsubishi (Zuba, MUZ-FS series), Daikin (Aurora series), LG (Red series cold-climate variants), Bosch (IDS 2.0 with vapor injection), Moovair (MCZ cold-climate mini-splits), Fujitsu (Halcyon XLTH), and a handful of others. Senville and some of the value brands have caught up in specs but lag in durability data. I take their -15C COP figures with a year's worth of forum reports before I trust them.

Mitsubishi Zuba is the long-time reference in Canadian installs. It has been the standard-bearer for the -25C club for over a decade. The Daikin Aurora series arrived in 2023. Bosch IDS 2.0 with the HVS vapor injection compressor landed in 2024.

## Field performance numbers

Field-tested COP at -10C outdoor, for well-installed cold-climate units, runs between 2.0 and 2.5 in published studies. At -20C, between 1.6 and 2.1. Below -20C the COP slides toward 1.4 to 1.6 and resistive backup kicks in for the difference between what the unit can deliver and what the house needs.

A serious cold-climate install in a cold US climate (six thousand degree days) will spend roughly 80 percent of its winter hours at temperatures above -10C, where the heat pump runs beautifully. The remaining 20 percent includes the ten to twenty coldest hours a year, where backup is real and useful, not a footnote.

## Backup heat, the part installers hide

Two ways to add backup. Resistive electric strips inside the indoor air handler. They are cheap to install, expensive to run. A 10 kW strip draws 10 kW. At $0.20 per kWh that is $2 per hour. Over a 50-hour cold snap that is $100 of pure resistance heat, on top of the heat pump's own running cost.

Dual fuel. The heat pump coil sits above a small gas furnace in the same ductwork. The control board swaps whichever fuel is cheaper right now. Setup is more involved, wiring-wise. Backup cost is whatever the gas bill comes to for the coldest stretch. Most cold-climate dual-fuel installs end up running the furnace 50 hours a year.

If your contractor says "you do not need backup in this climate," get a second opinion. Code requires backup in most cold US regions because of the worst-day scenario. Even if the heat pump can technically carry the load, the worst-case power outage means you have nothing. Backup is insurance, not an operating cost issue.

## The single most useful spec sheet line

Every cold-climate heat pump has a one-page AHRI certificate. The certificate is free. Search the AHRI directory by model number. Find the row that says "Maximum Heating Capacity at 5F / -15C" or similar. If that number is at least 70 percent of the nameplate cooling capacity, the unit is genuinely cold-climate. If the number is missing from the sheet, the manufacturer did not test it. Walk away.

The number is what you will have coming out of the unit when it is -15C out and the house most needs heat. Compared to the nameplate claim, this is the honest number. Ask about it by name. The right installer will produce it without blinking.
