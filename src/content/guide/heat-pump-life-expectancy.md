The honest range for a modern inverter heat pump installed in 2026 is 15 to 20 years. Beyond 18 years requires good maintenance and possibly luck. Below 12 years usually traces back to install errors, not brand defects.

## What pushes life expectancy up

Three things that correlate across long-life units in field reports.

Annual refrigerant pressure check. Units that get the yearly pressure visit tend to make it to year 19 and beyond. Refrigerant leaks are caught while small, brazed joints stay dry, compressor wear remains low.

Properly sized install. Units sized by Manual J rather than rule of thumb short-cycle less, log fewer equipment hours per year at the same comfort level. Accrue fewer hours on compressor and inverter board over 15 years.

Clean filter routine. Indoor coil and outdoor coil maintained at design airflow. Spec sheet COP roughly retained. Fewer freeze-thaw cycles on the indoor coil.

## What pushes it down

Three things commonly shorten heat pump life.

Coastal salt spray. Aluminum fins on the outdoor coil typically die in 8 to 12 years when the unit sits within 300 feet of an ocean shore. Halving the coil surface area through corrosion cuts airflow significantly and ages the compressor.

Oversized install. Short-cycling ages the unit. Each start-stop cycle stresses the compressor roughly 30 equivalent run-minutes. Six short cycles per hour accumulates faster than one hour continuous operation.

Line-set vacuum done wrong. Atmospheric moisture in the refrigerant loop decomposes into hydrofluoric and hydrochloric acids. The acids eat the inverter compressor windings. Slow degradation for 6 to 10 years until abrupt compressor failure.

## Aging symptoms

The unit runs more, delivers the same BTUs. Monthly bill rises faster than rate inflation. Noises during defrost become louder and longer. Compressor short-cycles more on minimum settings.

If you notice the same comfort for higher kWh per month, the COP has crept down by 10 to 20 percent. A quality service call with manifold test usually tracks it to either refrigerant leak or inverter wear. Age status of the inverter board, refrigerant sampled, and a COP test under known loads gives you a 5-year projection.

## The cost of long life

Maintenance cost of $200 to $400 per year adds roughly $4,000 over 18 years. Compared to the cost of replacing the compressor once prematurely at year 8 ($4,000 to $6,000), the maintenance pays for itself after one prevented failure.

Passive positioning. Do not place the unit against the side of the house. Do not let shrubs grow around the coil. Do not let snow melt flow under the unit every winter. Pad placed on the side of the house opposite the bedrooms, with concrete pad at grade, is the longest-lasting placement biome.

## A pattern in field data

Treat it like a car. Filter is the oil change. Refrigerant is the timing belt. Coil clean is the brake job. Skip these and the unit ages faster than expected. Skip them all and the unit fails around year 9 to 11 instead of year 17 to 19.

The maintenance bill comes annually. The installed unit is a 15-year relationship, and the relationship is the longest maintenance plan you have ever paid for a single piece of equipment in your house. Act accordingly.
