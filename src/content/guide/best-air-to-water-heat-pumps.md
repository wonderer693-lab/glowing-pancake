Air-to-water heat pumps are untrendy in the US, the default in northern Europe, the only choice if your house was built around radiators. They make hot water at 45 to 55C and run it through the same loops your boiler ran through. The principle is old. The product list is shorter.

## The four serious picks

### Mitsubishi EcoDan (PUZ-WM series)

Made its name in the retrofit markets of UK and Quebec. COP at -8C is 2.0 when supplying 50C water. A two-cylinder monoblock for outside, an indoor unit with hydraulic group and buffer. Serviceable by HVAC companies that already know Mitsubishi's refrigerant side. The go-to product for an "I do not want to take risks" hydronic retrofit.

Installed cost lands $20,000 to $30,000 for a 3-bedroom retrofit with new radiators and a 250 liter hot water cylinder. UK local authority BUS grant reduces £7,500 of that.

### Daikin Altherma 3 R32

Daikin's flagship air-to-water. The 3 model is the integrated cylinder variant, which is one box that contains everything but the outdoor unit. COP at -8C, 50C flow, around 2.2.

Cost lands $25,000 to $35,000 installed for the same 3-bedroom retrofit. Higher than EcoDan but compresses more of the inside house footprint. Choice between EcoDan and Altherma 3 tends to be about indoor space as much as about COP.

### Viessmann Vitola 200 (Vitocal Air-Water)

The German option, robust spec sheet, no US service network outside major metros. Vitola is a real retrofit-grade unit. COP nearly matches the Altherma 3 at 50C flow. Heat exchanger engineered for 5C outdoor at -25C, a rare honesty in high-temperature supply units.

In 2026 Viessmann has been quietly acquired by Carrier, and the residential air-to-water product line has been restructured. Service depends very much on your local Carrier reseller. Worth checking before buying.

### SpacePak Sim-HP

Made in the US and built to package a hydronic heat pump into the smallest indoor footprint. Sim-HP is the model. Outdoor monoblock plus a small inside module that has a buffer tank, manifold, and controls integrated. For North American retrofits where indoor space is tight, this is the right product, sometimes the only product. COP at -8C is 1.9 to 2.1 depending on flow temperature.

Built for North American installers who are not fluent in the hydronic retrofit world. The packaging saves installation time, which is where the hydronic retrofits usually blow budget.

## The two budget tier picks

### Chiltrix CX34

A modular outdoor air-to-water heat pump that you can add zones to. COP at -8C 50C is 1.95. Designed for sophisticated buyers who want to tinker. It is a tinkerer unit. Documentation pages exist that tell you exactly how to plumb the buffer tank, the buffer tank delta-T, the flow rate. This is not normal customer documentation, and a Chiltrix install by an unfamiliar installer usually fails the first time.

If you like the project, Chiltrix is good hardware. If you do not, it is the wrong buy. Pricing lands around $8,000 to $12,000 for the equipment, before install.

### Arctic Heat Pump 045A

Made in Canada, sold at a Canadian price point. The COP at -8C 50C is around 1.8. The service network is web-document based, sparse.

For houses with a willing owner and a small budget, this can be the difference between doing the retrofit and not. I would not recommend it for a retrofit where you depend on the unit absolutely through the winter. As a secondary system, in a milder climate, or for a hot-tub system, fine.

## Spec sheet red flags for air-to-water

Two flags to look for.

First. The unit publishes a COP at 35C flow and no COP at 50C flow. Lower flow temperature means higher COP, and 35C is the floor. The relevant number for radiators is 50C. If the brand only publishes 35C, get suspicious.

Second. The unit sold as monoblock but the spec sheet talks about outdoor refrigerant lines. Monoblock means all refrigerant is sealed inside the outdoor unit. Split space-to-water has the refrigerant lines between outdoor and indoor, and you need an F-gas certified installer for those. The DIY myth here is dense. Monoblock can be installed by a plumber with a heat pump ticket. Split needs refrigeration certification.

## The honest mead note about retrofit math

In any retrofit situation, the cost of the new radiators or underfloor modifications often exceeds the cost of the heat pump itself. A cold-climate EcoDan outdoor unit costs around $9,500 for the hardware. New steel panel radiators for a 3-bedroom house cost $4,000 to $7,000 for parts alone. Add the installer time to bleed, mount, plumb, and you can be at $11,000 just for radiator replacement.

When people tell me air-to-water is too expensive, what they usually mean is the radiator cost. The heat pump itself is decently priced. The decision whether to retrofit is more about your house plumbing than it is about heat pump brand. Take that question seriously before you sign anything.
