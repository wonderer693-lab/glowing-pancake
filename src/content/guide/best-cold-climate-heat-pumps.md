Cold-climate heat pumps are not all heat pumps. Most air-source units sold in the US today are sold for moderate temperatures. They quote heating capacity at 8.3C (47F), the AHRI standard point, and stop publishing numbers below -8C. If you live anywhere cold, you need the cold-climate subset of products. This is the short list.

## Selection criteria

Three things matter, in order.

1. COP at -8C above 1.8. This is the operating efficiency on the coldest average winter day. Below 1.8, the unit is barely better than resistance electric.
2. Maximum heating capacity at -15C above 70 percent of nameplate. This is whether the unit can still carry the house at design temperature, or has dropped the load and called backup.
3. Demand defrost, not timed defrost. The defrost cycle reverses the unit to melt frost off the outdoor coil. Timed defrost triggers every 90 minutes whether frost exists or not, dumping cold air. Demand defrost uses a coil temperature sensor and only defrosts when actually needed.

If a unit misses any of these in the spec sheet, it is not cold-climate regardless of what the website says.

## The picks

### Mitsubishi MUZ-FS HyperHeat (M-ZZ family)

The reference unit. COP 2.2 to 2.25 at -8C. Capacity at -15C at 75 to 82 percent of nameplate down to 2 tons, sliding to 70 percent at 4 tons. Demand defrost standard across the line. Ten-year parts and compressor warranty if installed by a Diamond Elite dealer and registered online.

The brand that taught North America what cold-climate meant. I have visited a half-dozen field-installed units across Maine, New Hampshire, New Brunswick and they generally meet spec. If the budget allows and the nearest installer is willing to come out for service, this is the default pick.

### Daikin Aurora (AURORA-M series)

Aurora is the North American cold-climate cousin of Daikin's European Altherma R32 line. COP 1.95 at -8C. Capacity at -15C around 70 percent. Demand defrost. Twelve-year parts warranty when installed by a Daikin Comfort Pro and registered.

Slightly behind Mitsubishi on the cold extreme but ahead on integrated smart controls. Daikin's One+ thermostat is one of the few consumer-grade products that properly handles switchover for dual fuel. Aurora lives in the same neighborhood as Mitsubishi on cost, around 3 to 5 percent less.

### Bosch IDS 2.0 with HVS vapor injection

The 2.0 had a tough cold-climate reputation for its first three years. The 2024 addition of HVS vapor injection compressors materially changed the picture. COP at -8C is now 1.95 to 2.05. Capacity at -15C is 70 percent or above, depending on outdoor unit size. Demand defrost uses the same sensor profile as Mitsubishi's, reasonably.

The ten-year parts and compressor warranty stays as Bosch's best talking point. Service network is thinner than Mitsubishi's in small metros, but in major metros with a Bosch channel partner it is no longer a bet. Installed cost runs 15 to 25 percent under Mitsubishi for matched specs.

### Fujitsu Halcyon XLTH

Long-time competitor to Mitsubishi in cold-climate mini-splits. XLTH coils are slightly heavier. COP at -8C around 2.1. Capacity at -15C around 78 percent. Demand defrost. Field longevity is comparable to Mitsubishi.

The reason XLTH does not own this list is that Fujitsu's North American service has been spottier since 2023 corporate restructuring. The units are good. The service backlog in some regions is longer than you want to ride out at -20C outside. Get a local Fujitsu installer's references from a cold-climate neighbor before committing.

### Moovair MCZ

The sleeper. Moovair is a Canadian cold-climate brand sourced from Mitsubishi Heavy Industries air conditioner systems. The MCZ multi-zone line uses the same compressor family as the MUZ-FS, in different packaging, at lower install cost when bought through Canadian distributor channels.

COP and cold performance match the MUZ-FS. Warranties are similar. Service network is smaller. For installs where the Diamond dealer install cost is too rich for the budget, Moovair is the cheap path to a real cold-climate product instead of a fake one.

## What I would skip in cold-climate installs

Anything sold by a brand with no published COP at -8C.

Anything described as "hybrid" when the spec sheet actually describes a single-stage on-off unit with resistive backup. This was the cheap cold-climate architecture before inverter compressors arrived, and it is still sold to people who do not read.

Anything the contractor refuses to pair with the manufacturer's demand-defrost control but sells as cold-climate anyway.

## The honest note

The four units above will all hold your house to 21C at -15C when sized correctly. The choice among them is not capacity, it is which installer you can phone at 8pm on the coldest January night when something has gone wrong. Pick the brand with the strongest service network in your specific ZIP code. That is the answer the internet cannot give you that the next-door neighbor with a ten-year-old Mitsubishi unit can.
