Three numbers describe a heat pump. COP is the one that actually matters. HSPF2 is the marketing number. SEER2 is the cooling number, relevant only if your summer matters.

## COP, the honest one

COP is the ratio of heat delivered to electricity consumed. COP 3 means 3 units of heat per 1 unit of electricity. The other 2 units came from the outdoor air or the ground. No free lunch is involved.

A heat pump spec sheet usually lists COP at several outdoor temperatures. 8.3C (47F), -8C (17F), sometimes -15C (5F). Look for the -8C and -15C numbers. These define worst-winter behavior. The 8.3C number is shoulder-season behavior and is irrelevant to anyone considering a unit for cold-climate heating.

A good cold-climate unit publishes COP at -15C around 1.8 to 2.3. Units that do not publish a -15C COP usually do not have a good number to publish.

The single best test question for any contractor. "Tell me the COP at -8C for this unit." If they cannot answer from memory, they have not installed that unit before in your climate. Either they open the spec sheet up and find it, or you ask for a different contractor.

## HSPF2, the marketing number

HSPF2 stands for Heating Seasonal Performance Factor, version 2. Higher is better. It is the total BTU output of the heat pump over a simulated heating season, divided by total watt-hours of electricity used.

The number is calculated by combining efficiency at several temperatures, weighted to a notional climate. The weighting assumes a moderate US climate, which makes HSPF2 a useful comparison number for southern and mid-Atlantic installs, and a misleading number for cold-climate installs.

For cold-climate installs, HSPF2 above 10 is a reasonable target. Anything above 12 is good. Below 8 is a sign your unit is not cold-climate and should not be considered.

The 2023+ federal minimum for split-system heat pumps is 7.5 HSPF2. Most value-brand units land at 8 to 9. Top cold-climate units land in 10 to 12. Squeezing beyond 12 requires money that rarely gets paid back at residential scale.

## SEER2, the cooling number

SEER2 measures seasonal cooling efficiency. Same idea as HSPF2 but for the air-conditioning side of the heat pump. Since most heat pumps also cool, SEER2 matters for half the year in humid climates and barely matters in coastal cool climates.

A SEER2 of 16 is reasonable. Above 20 is excellent. Above 25 is marketing fluff for residential installs. The jump from 16 to 20 saves maybe 20 percent on the cooling bill, which is a small dollar number for a small home but adds up for a large house in Texas.

The federal minimum SEER2 is 14 in the South, 13.4 in the North, as of 2025. Most cold-climate brands quote 17 to 19 because their inverter compressors happen to deliver good SEER2 in passing.

## Why the three numbers BS you

The trap. Some salespeople quote HSPF2 as if it were COP. They list a 12 HSPF2 number and call the unit "COP 12," which is wrong by a ratio of about 3.4. HSPF2 to COP conversion is roughly `COP = HSPF2 / 3.412`. So 12 HSPF2 is really COP 3.5 at the simulated climate weighted point, which is not what the unit does at -15C.

Other salespeople quote both numbers side by side and act as if both increasing is symmetry. It is not. HSPF2 can rise because the unit is beefier at shoulder-season temperatures. COP at -15C can fall at the same time, because the beefier compressor stage is less efficient in deep cold.

The defense. Ask for the published AHRI certificate. It has all three numbers in the same row. It also has the size of the indoor coil, the outdoor coil, and the furnace match. Read it like a car's window sticker. Save a copy. Match the quote against it.

## Two practical rules

First. Compare units within a tier of similar COP at -8C, then split the decision with SEER2 if cooling costs matter in your climate. Cold-climate buyers should not pay extra for SEER2 over 20.

Second. The unit's HSPF2 means nothing in a cold climate unless your installer also does a proper heat loss survey and sizes the unit from there. A 12 HSPF2 oversized heat pump that short-cycles will deliver 9 HSPF2 of real-world behavior. The number on the box is only as good as the install that sits underneath it.
